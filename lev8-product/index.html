THE PROJECT

Defining how an
AI GTM teammate works.

Go-to-market work begins with a goal: find customers, connect with partners, start a conversation. But a goal rarely arrives as a complete search specification.

Lev8 explores how AI can bridge that gap. My work spans the product model, the collaboration between users and AI, and the interfaces that carry a task from its first prompt to a usable result.

This case study presents the evolution of the design. Product screens contain sample data; they are not evidence of business performance.

01 / PRODUCT DEFINITION

The task was bigger
than the search.

The early model asked users to describe their profile and who they wanted to find. The product returned people or companies.

That model made search the center of the experience. The broader design opportunity was to help users turn an incomplete business goal into a sequence of decisions and actions.

THE DESIGN QUESTION

“Help me connect with 10 potential partners within the next two weeks.”

Who counts as a partner?
What makes them relevant?
What should happen after finding them?

A goal needs a workflow.

02 / AI INTERACTION MODEL

Ask for what is missing.
Keep the goal in view.

A natural-language entry point only helps if users do not have to know how to write the perfect prompt.

I shaped the onboarding around progressive clarification: understand the person, establish product context, then refine the target. The design annotations make the important exception explicit: when the query is already complete, move directly to the search plan.

01

Make the question answerable

Offer concrete choices, with an “Other” option for intent that does not fit a predefined category.

02

Preserve momentum

Show progress through the questions and allow skipping. Clarification should help the task advance.

03

Carry context forward

Reflect the answers in the conversation so the transition to a search plan stays understandable.

USER GOALEnough context?

Yes → Build a search plan

Not yet → Ask a focused question → Refine

03 / TRANSPARENCY & CONTROL

Make the plan reviewable.
Make execution legible.

The handoff to AI is a design moment. Users need to understand what will happen before committing to it.

The Search Plan turns an interpretation into something concrete: filters, enrichment, target matches, and estimated credits. The user can review and adjust that scope before running the search.

The plan is the agreement.

Natural language makes it easy to express intent. A structured plan makes it easier to spot an incorrect assumption.

  • FiltersWho should qualify?
  • EnrichmentWhat additional information is needed?
  • Target matchesHow much work should the search produce?
  • Estimated creditsWhat is the cost of this scope?
AI proposes.
The user adjusts.
Then the system executes.
DURING EXECUTION

Progress has
different levels of detail.

The streaming explorations include collapsed, hover, and expanded tool-call states. This creates a path from a quiet overview to more detail about the work in progress.

The design challenge is balancing visibility with attention: enough information to orient the user, without making every operation the main event.

04 / END-TO-END EXPERIENCE

The conversation starts it.
The workspace carries it.

A useful result needs a life beyond the chat that produced it.

I connected the conversational experience to persistent work objects: lists, reports, and documents. The split workspace keeps the dialogue available while the user inspects and acts on the result.

DIFFERENT GOALS, DIFFERENT ARTIFACTS

The goal shapes
the output.

A conversation can produce more than a table. Finding potential partners calls for a list to compare and act on. Understanding a company calls for a report that brings context, analysis, and evidence together.

I designed the workspace around the user’s purpose, with room for lists, reports, and other document formats. The conversation stays alongside the artifact so users can ask follow-up questions and refine the work.

DESIGN DECISION

Keep the interaction model consistent. Let the format adapt to what the user needs to understand or do next.

01 / CONTEXT

Keep the why close.

The conversation preserves the request and refinements behind the result.

02 / OBJECT

Give output a home.

Lists and reports become identifiable objects that can be opened and revisited.

03 / ACTION

Support the next move.

Enrich, export, or start outreach from the work already assembled.

CLOSING THE LOOP

From a relevant list
to a considered next step.

Finding people is only part of the original goal. Outreach connects discovery to action.

The flow moves through selecting a list, selecting contacts, writing an email, and choosing a channel. The drafting experience combines conversation with a reviewable email sample, so users can adjust the message before generating the full set.

THE DESIGN TRADEOFF

AI drafting, a user-written template, and template polishing offer different balances of effort, control, and credit cost. The experience makes that choice visible instead of treating automation as the only path.

REFLECTION

The design work was
in the transitions.

From an incomplete goal to shared context. From a proposed plan to execution. From AI output to a work object someone can act on.

That is the throughline of my contribution: helping define the product model, shaping how people collaborate with AI, and connecting individual interfaces into an end-to-end experience.

What the design establishes

A coherent interaction model connecting clarification, planning, execution, artifacts, and outreach.

What needs validation

Whether people can reach a useful list with less friction, understand credit costs, and confidently take the next action.

No quantified impact is claimed here. Evaluating this direction would require task completion, plan-editing, result-quality, and outreach-conversion evidence.
